Every great spirits house is expected to publish a thoughtful statement about its responsibilities to the earth, the community, and future generations. We take that expectation seriously. We've had a lot of time to think about future generations. Most of us are someone's past one.

## Carbon

La Cantina del Último Trago has been carbon neutral since roughly the moment the staff stopped breathing.

The cantina has no delivery fleet, no factory, no corporate jet, and no supply chain anyone can follow. Guests arrive by their own route, usually without meaning to, and the house does not count their journey against its footprint because, strictly speaking, nobody has ever been able to locate the house.

**Footprint:** None. The staff leave no footprints. We checked the dance floor. Bootprints, yes. Footprints, no.

## Water

Agave is one of the most water-wise plants on earth. We are one of the most water-wise spirits houses in any realm, in that we have used none.

Our position on water has been printed at the bottom of every page for years: **Save Water, Drink Tequila.** We stand by it, with one amendment from the responsibility committee: *drink actual water too.* The committee is Padre Proof. He was very firm about it.

## Agave Stewardship

Every bottle in the lineup begins in the blue agave fields along the desert road, where **El Vigía** watches the plants after midnight. No agave has been harvested in the production of any RIP Tequila expression, which makes ours the lowest-impact harvest in the history of the category.

The century plant behind **Don Muerte's** chair has survived three revolutions and seven competing origin stories. It receives one glass of water a week, one toast a night, and a level of respect most living things never get.

## Packaging

Our bottles are, and will remain, completely empty. Nothing to recycle. Nothing to discard. Nothing to break on the patio during the second chorus, which **Cantina Jack** considers the single greatest packaging innovation since the cork.

## Heritage

We honor tradition in everything we do: the ofrenda by the door, the marigolds along the bar, pan de muerto on the counter, and a candle for everyone who isn't here tonight. The cantina doesn't celebrate the dead because it's on brand. It celebrates the dead because, on most nights, the dead are the ones throwing the party.

## Community

The cantina is a place where every guest, from every century, gets the same welcome and the same pour. We do not have a VIP section. **Campana Roja** has been asked to create one and has refused on the grounds that everyone who gets past the bell is already very important.

Our community programs include:

- **The Empty Chair.** One seat at every table is kept open for someone who couldn't make it. Somebody always sits there eventually.
- **The Designated Mourner Initiative.** Guests who stay sober to drive their friends home receive a free zero-proof ritual and a permanent spot on the guest list. Jack keeps track. He never forgets.
- **The Excuse Amnesty.** Once a year, guests may retrieve one excuse from the fencepost vault, no questions asked. Nobody has ever taken the offer. They prefer to leave them where they are.

## Governance

The house is governed by a council of eight permanent residents, each holding one vote, with **Don Muerte** retaining one ceremonial veto per evening. He has never used it. Its value has increased every year.

Independent oversight is provided by **Padre Proof**, whose ledger has disagreed with Cantina Jack's tab for two hundred and fourteen consecutive years. We consider this the most robust system of financial checks and balances in the hospitality industry. Neither record has ever been wrong. Neither has ever matched.

Ethical review is conducted by a candle.

## Our Promise to Future Generations

We promise to keep the bell polished, the candle lit, the plant watered, and a seat open at the bar for every one of you. Take your time getting here. The cantina isn't going anywhere, and neither, eventually, are you.
