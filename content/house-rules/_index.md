The rules hang on a plank above the long mahogany bar, burned into the wood with a branding iron nobody admits to owning. Some have been amended. None have been repealed. **Cantina Jack** will point at the plank when necessary. He will not read it to you.

## The Thirteen Rules

### I. One toast per night.

**Don Muerte** gives it. You raise your glass. If you were planning a toast of your own, write it on a napkin and give it to **Padre Proof**, who will bless it and file it with the others. There are eleven thousand napkins in the booth. The booth is fine.

### II. No clocks inside the arch.

Watches are checked at the door with **El Vigía**, who does not give out claim tickets. Phones may stay in your pocket, but you will notice they now display the time as *later*.

### III. The bell is not a doorbell.

Do not ring it. Do not touch the ribbon. Do not ask **Campana Roja** when she plans to ring it, or why, or whether she could ring it a little early because your cousin is leaving. She knows about your cousin.

### IV. No requests.

**La Rosa Negra** has not taken a request in living memory, or the other kind. If you shout the name of a song, she will sing a different song, slower, about someone who shouted.

### V. No sitting during the second chorus.

This is not a rule so much as a forecast. **Los Maracas** have never lost a room. Benches are provided for standing on.

### VI. The tab is the tab.

Jack keeps one version. Padre keeps another. Neither will be shown to you, and neither will ever match. Both are correct. Settle up in stories.

### VII. Confess in the booth, not at the bar.

The bar is for pouring. The booth is for absolution. If you confess at the bar, Jack will nod with deep compassion and change nothing.

### VIII. What gets buried stays buried.

Arguments, bad texts, unsolicited opinions about the jukebox, and anything said between 1:00 and 3:00 AM go to **El Sepulturero**. He will give you a receipt. Do not try to dig anything back up. He knows where everything is, which is exactly why you can't have it.

### IX. Respect the plant.

The century agave behind Don Muerte's chair is older than every story about it. Do not lean on it, prune it, photograph it with the flash on, or ask which century.

### X. No dying on the dance floor.

You may only do it once, and most of the regulars have already used theirs. The rest of you are on probation.

### XI. Tip your bartender in secrets.

Cash is accepted but confusing. A good secret, told well, will cover a round. A great one covers the table. Jack has never repeated one, so tell him the real version.

### XII. Leave your excuse at the fencepost.

Every excuse for being out this late is collected at the road. They are kept, catalogued, and occasionally read aloud at staff meetings. *"I was just passing through"* has been submitted four hundred thousand times. It is still the house favorite.

### XIII. Toast the living.

The last rule is the only one Don Muerte wrote himself. Everybody here has already had their ending. Yours is still out there, which makes you the most interesting person in the room. Act accordingly.

## Amendments

The rules may be amended once a century by unanimous vote of the eight. The last session ran four nights and produced a single change: Rule XI was revised from *"in silver"* to *"in secrets"* after Jack pointed out that he had nowhere to put the silver.

A motion to allow requests on Tuesdays was tabled in 1958. It is still on the table. Rosa sets her drink on it.

> *"The rules are short because the night is long."* — Don Muerte
