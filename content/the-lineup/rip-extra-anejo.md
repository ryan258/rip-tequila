Some nights are no longer nights. By the time the Extra Añejo comes off the shelf, the room has settled. The toasts are quieter. The music has slowed. Somebody has already decided that this is the round they'll remember, even if the morning doesn't agree.

This is the bottle that handles those rounds. Long-aged, deep, and unflinching — a pour for stories you don't tell in mixed company and decisions you don't bring up at brunch. It doesn't celebrate the night. It witnesses it.

> *"Some Nights Deserve a Shovel."*

### A note from the caretaker

> *"Here lies your judgment."* — El Sepulturero

He pours, he steps back, and he lets the round find its own gravity. Don't ask him to join you. He's already keeping the records.

### Concept tasting notes: What's in the glass

Dark oak, dried fruit, cocoa, and a slow column of smoke. Roasted agave deep in the back. Leather. A finish that lingers like a name you weren't supposed to remember but did anyway. Best at a small table, late, with one or two people who already know the story.

The bottle doesn't soften over the night. You do.
