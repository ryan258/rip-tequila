There is a jukebox in the corner of the cantina. It has never been plugged in. It plays anyway, but only between sets, only at a volume just below conversation, and only songs that make you think about one specific person.

**La Rosa Negra** has never written her setlist down. There are no recordings, no live streams, and no encore negotiations. What follows was pieced together by regulars over many decades, each of whom remembers the songs perfectly and the order not at all.

## Side A · The Midnight Set

### 1. *Vela en la Orilla* (Candle on the Edge)

Always first. Rosa lights the candle on the edge of the stage, waits until every glass in the room stops moving, and begins with a single held note that lasts long enough for at least one person to text an ex. The text is never sent. El Sepulturero sees to that.

### 2. *El Pretendiente que No Llegó* (The Suitor Who Never Arrived)

The one about the rose. She sings it facing the door. Every night, someone in the back row turns around to check. Nobody is there. Somebody always checks.

### 3. *Tres de la Mañana* (Three in the Morning)

A slow bolero about clarity arriving far too late to be useful. Played at midnight, out of spite, so you can feel three o'clock coming.

### 4. *No Me Pidas Otra* (Don't Ask Me for Another)

Her only song about requests. It is four minutes long and each of the four minutes is somehow a refusal. Guests who shout a song title during this one are moved to the patio by consensus.

### 5. *Ceniza y Terciopelo* (Ash and Velvet)

The torch song. The tough guys cry here. The tough guys always say it's the smoke from the candle. The candle does not smoke.

## Side B · The Argument

At some point in the set, **Los Maracas** decide the room has been sad for long enough. Rosa has never agreed with them about when that is. The second half of the night is the sound of that disagreement, settled live, at tempo.

### 6. *Ya Basta, Rosa* (That's Enough, Rosa)

Los Maracas begin playing while Rosa is still holding the last note of the previous song. It is technically a cumbia. It is emotionally an intervention.

### 7. *El Trompetista Caliente* (The Hot Trumpet)

An instrumental. The trumpet has not cooled down since 1952 and this is the song that keeps it that way. Jack moves three more bottles to the bottom shelf when he hears the first bar.

### 8. *Bailando en la Banca* (Dancing on the Bench)

The second chorus of this one is where Rule V of the House Rules comes from. Nobody sits. Nobody has ever sat. A guest tried in 1987 and the bench stood up.

### 9. *Huesos Felices* (Happy Bones)

Pure joy, at a tempo regulars describe as *medically inadvisable*. Somebody in a bolo tie will invite you to dance. You'll say no. Then you'll say yes. That's the song.

### 10. *La Revancha de la Rosa* (The Rose's Revenge)

Rosa wins the argument. She doesn't stop them. She just starts singing over the top of the cumbia, slower, sadder, and in a different key, and somehow they follow her. The whole room dances to a song about heartbreak and nobody can explain how that happened.

## The Last Song

### 11. *Hasta la Próxima Vida* (Until the Next Life)

The closing number. Never announced, never listed, never the same song twice according to anyone who has heard it. When it begins, **Campana Roja** takes her hand off the cord, **Don Muerte** sets down the glass he never drank from, and **El Sepulturero** quietly picks up his spade.

People say it's about goodbye. Rosa says it's about the people who stayed until the end, which is a different thing entirely.

## Jukebox Selections Between Sets

The unplugged jukebox has a playlist of its own. Nobody stocked it. Guests report hearing:

- *That Song*, which is not a title. It's whatever song you were hoping you wouldn't hear tonight.
- *A Waltz for the Driver*, which only plays when someone at the table has agreed to stay sober. El Vigía is believed to be responsible.
- *Silence, Extended Mix*, which plays when Don Muerte stands up to give the toast.
- *Bell Interlude*, a single chime that lasts nine seconds. It only plays at 9:42 PM. Campana Roja denies all involvement and has never once been near the jukebox at 9:42 PM.
