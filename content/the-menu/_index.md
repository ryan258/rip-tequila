**Cantina Jack** does not hand out menus. He considers them an admission that the bartender has stopped paying attention. What follows was copied from the inside of his bar towel by a regular with excellent eyesight and poor survival instincts.

Measurements are approximate. Jack pours by feel, by mood, and by how long you've been staring at the bottles pretending to read them. Every recipe begins with the same instruction, which he has underlined twice: *Look the guest in the eye first.*

## Signature Cocktails

### The Bell Ringer

*RIP Reposado · Campana Roja · 9:42 PM*

- 2 oz RIP Reposado
- ¾ oz fresh lime
- ½ oz agave syrup
- 1 dash of hibiscus, for the ribbon
- A wheel of blood orange

Shake hard, strain over a single large cube, and float the blood orange like it was always going to be there. Do not drink it until the bell rings. If the bell does not ring, Jack will quietly take it back and give you water. He is not being cruel. He is being accurate.

> *"You don't order this one. It happens to you."* — Cantina Jack

### The Black Rose Sour

*RIP Reposado · La Rosa Negra · Just before midnight*

- 2 oz RIP Reposado
- 1 oz lemon
- ¾ oz blackberry syrup
- 1 egg white, or a convincing substitute
- 3 drops of bitters in the shape of a tear

Dry shake, shake again with ice, strain into a coupe, and set it down on the edge of the stage beside the candle. Rosa will not drink it. That isn't the point. The point is that someone thought of her, and she has been waiting decades for that.

### The Toast

*RIP Añejo · Don Muerte · 11:58 PM*

- 2 oz RIP Añejo
- 1 bar spoon of piloncillo syrup
- 2 dashes of chocolate bitters
- An orange peel, expressed with conviction

Stir until the room goes quiet. Strain over one cube. Hold it at shoulder height for the duration of the toast and do not sip until Don Muerte's glass comes down. Nobody has ever seen his come down. Use your judgment.

### The Absolution

*RIP Añejo · Padre Proof · Any hour, after the mistake*

- 1½ oz RIP Añejo
- ½ oz sweet vermouth
- ¼ oz cherry liqueur
- 1 brandied cherry, blessed

Stir, strain, and garnish with the cherry, which Padre Proof will bless whether or not you ask. Order this one *after* you've done the thing. Ordering it before is considered planning, and planning is not covered.

### The Shallow Grave

*RIP Extra Añejo · El Sepulturero · 1:43 AM*

- 2 oz RIP Extra Añejo
- ¼ oz mezcal, rinsed and discarded like an alibi
- 1 bar spoon of dark honey
- A dusting of cacao, like fresh earth

Rinse the glass with mezcal and throw the mezcal away. Stir the rest over ice, strain into the smoky glass, and dust the surface with cacao. El Sepulturero insists the drink is not named after anything specific. He insists this while holding a shovel.

### Earth-Rested Old Fashioned

*RIP Extra Añejo · Cantina Jack · Bottom shelf*

- 2 oz RIP Extra Añejo, from the bottle closest to the floor
- 1 sugar cube
- 2 dashes of aromatic bitters
- A splash of water from the patio well

Muddle, stir, strain over a large cube. The only cocktail on the menu built specifically from bottles that survived a Los Maracas downbeat. Jack claims the floor-level storage adds depth. The bottles claim nothing. They are still recovering.

### The Late Arrival

*Midnight Agave Reserve · El Vigía · 2:17 AM*

- 2 oz Midnight Agave Reserve
- Nothing else

It is already waiting when you sit down. You will not remember ordering it. You didn't. El Vigía sent it in from the road about an hour before you parked, and Jack poured it the moment the hat tipped.

> *"If you have to ask what's in it, it isn't yours."* — Cantina Jack

### Shake Now, Explain Later

*RIP Blanco · Los Maracas · Second chorus*

- 1½ oz RIP Blanco
- 1 oz pineapple
- ½ oz lime
- ½ oz jalapeño syrup
- A salted rim, applied mid-dance

Shaken by Los Maracas themselves, in rhythm, for exactly as long as the song lasts. Last Tuesday that was eleven minutes. The drink was fine. The trumpet player needed a moment.

### The Alibi

*RIP Blanco · Cantina Jack · 8:07 PM*

- 1½ oz RIP Blanco
- Soda water
- A long squeeze of lime
- Salt on half the rim, so you have options

The first drink of the night, built tall so it looks like you're being responsible. You told your friends you were only stopping by. This is the drink that agrees with you, for now.

## Zero-Proof Rituals

The cantina pours for the living, too. Jack makes these with exactly the same care, because the person who isn't drinking is usually the one who remembers the night correctly, and he respects a fellow witness.

### The Designated Mourner

- 4 oz tamarind agua fresca
- ½ oz lime
- A pinch of chile salt
- One sprig of mint, standing upright, keeping watch

Built over crushed ice for whoever is driving the hearse home. Comes with a nod from El Vigía and a permanent spot on the guest list.

### Rosa's Candle

- 3 oz hibiscus tea, chilled
- 1 oz blood orange
- ½ oz agave syrup
- Sparkling water, poured slowly, like a ballad

All the heartbreak of the Black Rose Sour with none of the morning. Rosa approves of this one, mostly because people who order it stay for the whole set.

### The Clean Slate

- Sparkling mineral water
- A lime wedge
- Total moral clarity

Served in the same heavy shot glass as everything else, so nobody at the table has to ask questions. Padre Proof considers it the only drink in the building that requires no absolution. He orders one at dawn.

## Bar Snacks

- **Pan de Muerto Croutons** — torn from the loaf on the ofrenda, with permission.
- **Chapulines al Limón** — crunchy, salted, and judging you only slightly.
- **The Shared Plate** — whatever El Sepulturero found in the back office, arranged nicely.
- **Lime Wheels, à la Carte** — Jack keeps a bowl. They are free. They are watched.

> *"I pour. You explain tomorrow."* — Cantina Jack

The bottles above live only behind the cantina's bar. If you're mixing at home with the regular kind, measure honestly, eat something, drink water between rounds, and get a ride. The [Responsible Language](/responsible-language/) page covers the rest.
